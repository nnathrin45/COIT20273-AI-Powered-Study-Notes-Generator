Cellular Respiration

Cellular respiration is the process by which cells release energy stored in glucose
and convert it into ATP, the molecule cells use for energy.

There are three main stages. Glycolysis occurs in the cytoplasm and breaks one glucose
molecule into two pyruvate molecules, producing a net gain of two ATP. The Krebs cycle,
also called the citric acid cycle, takes place in the mitochondrial matrix and releases
carbon dioxide while producing NADH and FADH2. The electron transport chain occurs across
the inner mitochondrial membrane and produces the majority of the ATP, around 34 molecules
per glucose.

Aerobic respiration requires oxygen and produces roughly 36 to 38 ATP per glucose molecule.
Anaerobic respiration occurs without oxygen and produces only two ATP, with lactic acid or
ethanol as a by-product.

Oxygen acts as the final electron acceptor in the electron transport chain. Without oxygen,
the chain stops and the cell must rely on anaerobic pathways.
